Research defense explainer

What Is a Mock Defense in Research?

A mock defense is a structured rehearsal before a formal research proposal, thesis or dissertation defense. You present the work, answer realistic follow-up questions and receive specific feedback. The session has no authority to approve, reject or grade the research.

Core distinction: a formal defense tests the work under your institution's rules; a mock defense tests your readiness while there is still time to repair weak reasoning, unclear explanations and practical problems.

What does “mock defense” mean?

The phrase describes a simulation, not one universal academic procedure. In a proposal context, the rehearsal tests whether the planned question, method, ethics and timetable are defensible. In a final thesis or dissertation context, it tests whether you can explain the contribution, evidence, limitations and implications of completed work.

Universities use different names and formats. UCL recommends mock-viva practice, the University of Calgary advises thesis candidates to do a mock exam with colleagues, and the University of Melbourne describes the goal as refining the ability to explain and justify research. These examples support rehearsal as a preparation method; they do not make one institution's process universal.

Which type of defense are you preparing for?

Research proposal defense

Your main job is to justify a study that has not yet been completed. Practise the problem, research question, method fit, ethics, access, feasibility, risks and alternative plans. Start with what a research proposal defense is.

Thesis or dissertation defense

Your main job is to defend completed work. Practise the central contribution, evidence, methodological choices, limitations, relationship to the literature and implications. If your institution calls the examination a viva, read what a mock viva is.

A useful 30-minute mock defense

  1. Minutes 0–3 — set the contract: name the real assessment, time limit and one question the rehearsal must answer.
  2. Minutes 3–10 — present: explain the problem, contribution, method, evidence or feasibility, and the most important boundary.
  3. Minutes 10–22 — defend: answer broad, specific and follow-up questions without seeing them in advance.
  4. Minutes 22–27 — receive evidence-based feedback: record where the claim, evidence, reasoning or limitation became unclear.
  5. Minutes 27–30 — choose three repairs: assign one content repair, one answer repair and one delivery repair.

Weak feedback: “Be more confident.”

Actionable feedback: “When asked why you rejected the alternative method, you described your choice but did not compare the trade-offs. Add one sentence on what the alternative would improve and one on why it would weaken this study.”

How to judge whether it worked

Do not score success by comfort alone. A useful mock defense produces observable repairs. For example, you can now state the main contribution in two sentences, find supporting evidence quickly, answer a limitation without becoming defensive, and explain what you would change under a realistic constraint.

Retest the weakest answers with different wording. Repeating the same script can improve fluency while hiding whether the reasoning transfers to an unfamiliar question.

Limits and safe use

Confirm the real rules with your graduate school, programme handbook or examination office. Ask about presentation requirements, participants, timing, public or private sections, permitted materials, accessibility adjustments and possible outcomes. A rehearsal can reduce uncertainty and improve preparation, but it cannot predict questions, reproduce examiner behaviour or guarantee an outcome.

Frequently asked questions

What is a mock defense in research?

It is a rehearsal where a researcher presents and justifies planned or completed work, answers follow-ups and uses feedback to prepare for a formal defense.

Is a mock defense the same as the real defense?

No. It is practice and has no formal authority. Your institution controls the real examination.

What should happen after a mock defense?

Turn feedback into a short repair list, revise the weakest claims or answers, and test them again with new question wording.
